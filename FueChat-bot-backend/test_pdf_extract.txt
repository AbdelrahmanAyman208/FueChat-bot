4Introduction
The Faculty of Computers and Information Technology at Future University was established in 2006. Since its
establishment, the faculty has been working to achieve excellence in the fields of computing and informatics and to
keep pace with the continuous and rapid technological development in the job market.
Accordingly, stemming from its national responsibility and in pursuit of fulfilling its mission, the faculty is keen on
preparing generations equipped with the foundations of modern knowledge and fully ready to keep up with rapid
technological advancements. It also aims to develop students’ ability to achieve competitive quality by performing
their duties efficiently and sincerely. The curricula and courses offered are continuously evaluated, and the latest
educational systems are adopted to allow greater student participation and to open up wider opportunities for
students to choose courses according to their abilities, potentials, and interests.
Within this framework, this regulation has been developed in accordance with the latest developments in the labor
market and the advancements in the field of computing and informatics.

6Vision and Mission
University Vision
Future University aspires to achieve leadership locally and regionally and to become a pioneering model in education,
scientific research, and community service at both the national and international levels.
University Mission
Future University is committed to providing world-class quality educational services to prepare graduates with
scientific and innovative competencies that keep pace with labor market changes. It also aims to provide a
motivating environment for the advancement of scientific research and contribute to achieving sustainable
development goals and technological progress, while instilling professional values and ethics.
Collage Vision
Striving to achieve a distinguished position in the field of computing and informatics locally, regionally, and internationally.
Collage Mission
The Faculty of Computers and Information Technology at Future University in Egypt (FUE) is committed to providing a
distinguished educational environment to prepare qualified cadres in various fields of technology and software, capable of
competing and keeping pace with the continuous development in the local, regional, and international labor markets. It also
aims to produce outstanding scientific research that contributes to the generation of knowledge and advanced technology,
and to participate effectively in achieving sustainable development goals while adhering to core values and professional ethics.

ةحئﻼلاArticle (2): Study System at the College
 The study system is based on the credit hour system, where the credit hour is the unit of
study measurement used to determine the weight of a course.
 The academic year consists of two regular semesters (Fall and Spring semesters) and a
summer semester, which is optional for students. The summer semester is held according to
the college's capabilities and for a financial fee determined by the university.
 The duration of regular semesters is 16-17 weeks, including the exam period.
 The summer semester is an intensive semester where the weekly study hours for a course are
doubled. Its duration is 7-8 weeks, including the exam period.
 The maximum duration of study at the college is 8 academic years (16 regular semesters, Fall
and Spring), noting that approved suspension semesters, approved by the College Council
and the University Council, are not counted within the permitted semesters.
 Study at the college follows the credit hour system, where the total number of hours required
to obtain a bachelor's degree is 132 credit hours. Final-year students prepare a project
extending over two semesters. The specialized department councils determine its topic, and
the College Council, based on the proposal of the specialized department councils, sets an
additional period for the project after the written exam at the end of the academic year. It
also forms committees for the practical and oral evaluation of the projects.
 Language of Instruction: English.
 Definition of the Credit Hour: The student receives a number of credit hours according to the
number of contact hours per week. Contact hours are calculated as follows:
o One lecture hour counts as one credit hour.
o Two or three hours (tutorials or labs) count as one credit hour.
Article (3): Admission and Transfer Rules
Admission Requirements for the College
 The college accepts graduates of general secondary education specializing in Mathematics or
Sciences, or their equivalent from other accredited certificates, in the same academic year for
enrollment in the college, in accordance with the coordination rules of the Council of Private
Universities.
 Science stream students must pass the course equivalent to the Math 2 course intended for
Mathematics stream students in general secondary education.
12Collage Transfer Requirements
Students may be transferred to the college from other university faculties or similar or dissimilar
institutes, provided they have obtained the minimum total score required for admission to the
college, in a manner that does not conflict with the rules and regulations set by the Supreme Council
of Universities and the Council of Private and National Universities. In case a student's transfer from
a corresponding faculty is accepted, course equivalency (mapping) shall be conducted to equate the
courses they have studied with the courses offered by the department to which they are transferring,
subject to the approval of the College Dean and University President. Transfer from one
specialization to another within the college is permitted, provided the student meets the admission
requirements for the new specialization. Course equivalency requires that the student's passing
grade in the course is not less than 65% .

Article (5): Rules for Enrollment in Programs
The College Council determines the regulations governing the distribution of students among the
offered programs based on a proposal from the scientific department councils.
Article (6): Attendance Rules
Student entry to the final exam requires achieving an attendance rate of no less than 75% of the
lectures and tutorials in each course. If a student's absence rate in a course exceeds 25% without an
acceptable excuse, the College Council may deny them entry to the final exam, and they will receive a
grade of "zero" on the final exam score for that course. However, if the student submits an excuse
acceptable to the College Council (within the period specified by the council), a grade of "Withdrawn"
will be recorded for them in the course for which the excuse was submitted.

Article (14): Semester Average and Cumulative Average

The student's semester average (GPA) is calculated based on the sum of points the
student obtained in all courses they registered for in the semester, divided by
the total credit hours for those courses. The student's cumulative average
(CGPA) is calculated based on the sum of points the student obtained in all
courses they have studied, divided by the total credit hours for those courses.
The student's grades are recorded in their academic record each semester, along
with their semester and cumulative averages.
• The average shall be calculated to the nearest two decimal places.
18Article (15): Graduation Project
• Each scientific department proposes topics related to its specialization for which graduation
projects can be prepared and announces them to students. The graduation project is calculated as 6
credit hours over two consecutive regular semesters (Fall and Spring). The rules for registering for
the graduation project are as follows:
 A student who has successfully completed 93 credit hours of the total credit hours required
for graduation is entitled to register for the project course. A student expected to graduate
after studying a summer semester who has not yet completed 93 credit hours may register
for the project course after approval from the scientific department and the college.
• The minimum passing grade for the project course is 50% of the total course score, with the
project discussion grades comprising 40% .
Article (16): Academic Probation
• A student is placed on the academic probation list if their cumulative average falls below 2.00 (out
of 4.00), except for the first semester. The student and their guardian are notified in writing of the
probation status and its consequences. The academic probation is lifted if the student succeeds in
raising their cumulative average to at least (2.00) in the semester following the probation.
Article (17): Course Repetition
 • Repeating a course previously failed in:
 If a student fails a course, they must retake it and be examined in it again. If they pass the
course after retaking it, the actual grade they obtain is calculated for them, not
exceeding 83 (the highest grade in B).
 Their cumulative average is calculated on this basis, counting the course hours only once.
 All instances of repetition and the grade (or evaluation) obtained by the student in each
repetition appear on the student's detailed transcript.
• Repeating a course previously passed with points less than 2.00 to raise the cumulative GPA
to avoid dismissal:
 If a student has a cumulative GPA (CGPA) at the beginning of the semester of less than 2.00
(student under academic probation), they must raise their average.
 The student mentioned in the previous point (placed under academic probation) is entitled to
repeat any course they have previously passed (with points less than 2.00) to raise their
cumulative GPA to avoid dismissal. In this case, they must retake the course and be examined
in it again. They will receive the highest grade obtained from all repetition attempts, not
exceeding 83 (the highest grade in B). There is no maximum limit for these courses; rather, the student (placed under academic
probation) may repeat any number of courses they have previously passed (with points less
than 2.00) in order to raise their cumulative GPA (CGPA) to at least 2.00.
 The student's cumulative average is calculated on this basis, counting the course hours only
once.
 All instances of repetition and the grade (or evaluation) obtained by the student in each
repetition appear on the student's detailed transcript.
• Repeating a course previously passed with points greater than or equal to 2.00 to raise the
cumulative GPA for improvement:
 A student with a cumulative GPA greater than or equal to 2.00 is entitled to improvement to
raise their cumulative GPA, but only if they have not yet completed the requirements for
graduation.
 If a student wishes to repeat a course they have previously passed (with points greater than
or equal to 2.00) to raise their cumulative GPA, they must retake the course and be examined
in it again. In this case, they will receive the highest grade obtained from all repetition
attempts.
 The maximum number of hours permitted for improvement for courses in which the student
obtained points greater than or equal to 2.00 or higher is 9 hours, whether for a single
course or a number of courses.
 Their cumulative average is calculated on this basis, counting the course hours only once.
 All instances of repetition and the grade (or evaluation) obtained by the student in each
repetition appear on the student's detailed transcript.
Article (18): Summer Training
 Summer training is organized for a period of 6 weeks, with no less than 12 hours per week,
provided that the student has successfully completed at least 70 credit hours of the total
credit hours required for graduation.
 A faculty member is assigned as responsible for the training, along with a number of assistant
staff members, to follow up with the training participants annually and to assign evaluations
for each of them according to the criteria determined by the College Council.
20• The summer training course is compulsory for all students and is not counted within the
credit hours that enter into the calculation of the cumulative average. Rather, it is an acquired
hours among the graduation requirements. Therefore, the student's grade in it is Pass/Fail,
and it is mentioned in the student's detailed transcript.
Article (19): Dismissal from the College
• A student who receives an academic probation in 4 consecutive regular
semesters or 6 separate regular semesters (with the possibility that some may be
consecutive) is dismissed.
• A student is dismissed from the college if they exceed the maximum duration of study
at the college, after deducting the semesters of suspended enrollment.
• A student subject to dismissal for any of the aforementioned reasons may be given an
additional and final opportunity to register for two consecutive regular semesters in
addition to a summer semester to fulfill the graduation requirements, provided they have
successfully completed no less than 105 hours of the total hours required for graduation,
and subject to the approval of the College Council and the University.
Article (20): Student Grievances
• Any student has the right to submit a request to appeal their exam
result in one or more courses within a maximum period of one week
from the date of the results announcement.
• The student is notified in writing of the result of the examination of
their submitted grievance.
Article (21): Honors Rank and Student Ranking
• A student is granted honors rank if they successfully complete the courses they have
studied at each academic level with a grade of no less than Very Good (i.e., a
cumulative GPA CGPA of no less than 3.00), provided that the period of study does
not exceed 4 years (eight regular semesters), and that the student has not failed or
been denied entry in any course during their study at the college or the college from
which they transferred (if any).
• Students are ranked based on the cumulative GPA (CGPA). In case of a tie, they are
ranked according to the total sum of grades.

is removed by the Dean or the Head of the Examination Committee and is prohibited from
taking the exam.
 Disciplinary penalties are:
o Verbal or written warning.
o Probation.
o Deprivation of certain student services.
o Deprivation from attending classes of one or more courses for a period not exceeding
one month.
o Dismissal from the college for a period not exceeding one month.
o Deprivation from taking exams in one or more courses.
o Dismissal from the college for a period not exceeding one semester.
o Final dismissal from the university.
 The Dean of the college has the authority to impose the first six penalties after their approval
by the University President.
 The Disciplinary Council has the authority to impose all penalties. The decision to refer a
student to the Disciplinary Council is issued by the University President upon the request of
the Dean.
 The Student Disciplinary Council is formed as follows:
o The Dean of the College.
o The Vice Dean of the College.
o One of the faculty members of the college, chosen annually by the College Council.

English - 1 (ENG101) 1 - ة يزيلجنا ة غل
The course provides an academic reading and writing proficiencies that enabling students to build on
the reading and writing skills they have developed throughout the years and to progress toward an
advanced level of literacy. Topics covered in this course are reading comprehension, writing
fundamentals, essay writing, creative writing, vocabulary building, literary analysis and the core
transferable skills in critical thinking, reading and writing that students will use in their university
courses, in addition, analysis of a variety of challenging readings.
English - 2 (ENG102) 2 - ة يزيلجنا ة غل
The course provides a wide variety of reading and writing skills that develop the student's language
practice, in addition to longer reading materials that enhance understanding of various academic
topics that are considered the main focus of this course.Topics covered in this course are topical and
functional lexis provide the reference by which language is introduced and recycled within clear
natural contexts, writing is explored through a range of texts, by understanding genre specific
conventions, and developing confidence by planning and discussions and by applying both process
and product approaches.
Human Rights (PSC110) ن اسنﻹا ق وقح
The course provides the conceptual foundation of human rights, the development of human rights
framework and multi- disciplinary character of the field as an area of the study. Topics covered in this
course are the universal declaration on human rights, the international covenant on economic, social,
and cultural rights and all related agreements. It will also touch on the state of human rights in egypt
and the arab world.
Communication Skills and Report Writing (CPS121) ريراقتلا ةباتكو لصاوتلا تاراهم
The course provides students with abilities to effectively communicate and convey information
through well-structured reports and developing essential skills necessary for producing concise, clear,
and professional reports that cater to different audiences and purposes. Topics covered in this course
are the planning, drafting, editing, and presentation phases, organizing information logically,
employing appropriate language and tone. Also, covering both written and oral communications and
ensuring students can articulate their ideas effectively through written reports as well as presentations.

Mathematics and Basic Sciences
Calculus (BMT101) ل ماكتو لضافت
The course provides the fundamental principles and techniques of calculus, which is a branch of
mathematics that deals with rates of change and accumulation. Topics covered in this course are
functions, limits, continuity, differentiation and rules of differentiation, chain rule, implicit
differentiation, differentiation of parametric functions, transcendental functions and differentiation.
Also, the course covers trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions, exponential and logarithmic
functions, hyperbolic and inverse hyperbolic functions, indeterminate forms and l'hopital's rule,
application of derivatives, taylor and maclaurin expansion and extrema of a function, integration
concepts, transcendental functions and techniques of integration like integration by substitutions,
integration by parts and integration by partial fractions.
Differential Equations (BMT121) ة يلضافت تﻻداعم
The course provides students with a basic understanding of the fundamental theorem of calculus,
integration by trigonometric substitutions, integration by reduction , applications of definite integral:
area, volume, arclengh of a curve. polynomial and series. Topics covered in the course are testing for
convergence and divergence of a series, ordinary differential equations like equations of first order
(separable, homogenous, exact, linear and Bernoulli), orthogonal trajectories, equations reducible to

Visual Effects (DMT414) ة يرصب تارثؤم
The course provides the students with the art and technology of creating stunning visual effects for
film, television, and other media. Topics covered in this course are the fundamental principles and
techniques used in the visual effects industry including compositing, motion tracking, 3D animation,
and digital simulations, green screen compositing, particle effects, creature animation, and more.

Autodesk Maya, and Nuke, and anyother recent application work on practical projects to create
realistic visual effects sequences. Additionally, students will explore the integration of visual effects
with live-action footage and gain insights into the collaborative nature of visual effects production.

coding standards and video analytics for surveillance and computer vision applications. Through real-
world projects and case studies, students will develop a deep understanding of video processing
techniques and their role in modern multimedia and computer vision systems.
